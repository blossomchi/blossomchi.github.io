Building assistive & autonomous robotic systems for real-world impact

Research

Three systems that perceive, decide, act and then check the result. Each project page separates what has been measured from what is still planned.

Accepted

Gesture-controlled assistive arm for speech-impaired cancer patients

A bedside webcam reads three gestures (zoom, pan, highlight) and an Arduino-controlled arm repositions a medical display. The same network, CDAM-Net, screens histopathology patches and posts results to a clinician dashboard. It shares one ResNet50 backbone between two very different kinds of image and adapts features per task to avoid negative transfer.

Papers
Pilot study in the FNAS Journal of Computing and Applications; extended CDAM-Net paper at ICARM 2026, held at the University of Évry Paris-Saclay, France.
Evidence
85.0% gesture accuracy, 94.0% patch classification accuracy and 0.82 s mean latency across 100 trials with 10 healthy volunteers.
Scope
Laboratory simulation. No patients yet; clinical validation is the next step.

Accepted

Firefighting robot that checks whether its own spray worked

A wheeled base carries a two-axis pan/tilt nozzle arm. Camera, flame, heat, gas, distance, inertial and water-flow readings are fused with reliability weights. After each spray pulse the robot compares flame and heat readings before and after, and if the fire is not responding it re-selects the target and re-aims instead of spraying blindly.

Paper
Accepted at SCA 2026, the 11th International Conference on Smart Cities & Applications, Karabük University, Türkiye, 10 to 12 November 2026. Publication is planned in Springer's Lecture Notes in Networks and Systems.
Evidence
Simulation with released code. With sensor faults injected, reliability-weighted fusion detected the fire in 94% of fire runs with 5% false alarms. With a deliberately offset first aim, the full loop extinguished the simulated fire in 86% of 100 trials against 0% for the open-loop and adaptive-flow baselines.
Scope
The subsystems are built and being integrated. No hardware results are reported yet.

Accepted

Search-and-rescue planner for microcontroller-class robots

With only ultrasonic, motion, temperature, gas and inertial sensors, an ESP32 robot has to decide where to search next and which sensor is worth the battery. A Search Priority Score combines victim likelihood, hazard, navigation risk, distance and remaining charge, and an active-sensing rule spends energy only where the evidence is ambiguous.

Paper
Accepted for oral presentation at the International Conference on Cybersecurity & Artificial Intelligence (CSAI Days 2026), 29 October 2026.
Evidence
Simulation of 23,400 episodes across six scenarios and thirteen policies, with released code. Active sensing cut energy per verified detection by about 24% against always-sample fusion with no detectable change in detection or false-positive rate. Under a 30% battery cap, most of the survival advantage came from a simple reserve rule, not from the battery-dependent weights.
Scope
The platform has not been assembled yet. Results come from a documented synthetic sensor model and may shift on real hardware.
